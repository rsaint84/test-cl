## 2. Governing legal frameworks (plain summaries)

### California Labor Code § 925 (choice of law / forum)
An employer may not require an employee who primarily lives and works in California, as a condition of employment, to agree to litigate or arbitrate California claims outside California or to give up California law. A violating clause is voidable by the employee, and the matter is then heard in California under California law; the employee can recover attorney's fees. "Adjudication includes litigation and arbitration." Key exception: the section does not apply if the employee "is in fact individually represented by legal counsel in negotiating" the forum or choice-of-law term. Applies to contracts entered, modified, or extended on or after Jan. 1, 2017. Text: https://california.public.law/codes/ca_lab_code_section_925 ; summaries: https://www.kelleydrye.com/viewpoints/blogs/labor-days/new-california-law-prevents-employers-from-imposing-non-california-forum-selection-or-choice-of-law-provisions-upon-california-employees ; https://www.sheppard.com/insights/blogs/california-labor-code-section-925-a-word-of-caution-for-out-of-state-employers-of-california-employees

### California Labor Code §§ 2870–2872 (inventions)
§ 2870: an invention-assignment clause does not reach inventions the employee made entirely on their own time without employer equipment, supplies, facilities, or trade secrets, unless the invention relates to the employer's business or anticipated R&D, or results from work done for the employer; a clause reaching further is against public policy and unenforceable. § 2871: an employer may not require an employee to agree to a term void under § 2870, but may require disclosure of inventions and a review process. § 2872: if an agreement contains an assignment clause, the employer must give the employee written notice that it does not apply to § 2870 inventions. Sources: https://www.fbm.com/intellectual-property/publications/brilliant-idea-now-who-owns-the-patent/ ; https://sierraiplaw.com/employee-invention ; sample clause language: https://www.lawinsider.com/clause/labor-code-section-2870-notice

### Business & Professions Code §§ 16600, 16600.1, 16600.5 (non-competes; 2024 changes)
§ 16600 voids every contract that restrains anyone from engaging in a lawful profession, trade, or business, with narrow sale-of-business exceptions. Edwards v. Arthur Andersen (2008) read it to bar even narrow post-employment non-competes. Effective Jan. 1, 2024, § 16600.1 (AB 1076) makes it unlawful to include a non-compete in an employment agreement or require one, and required individualized written notice by Feb. 14, 2024 to current and former employees (employed after Jan. 1, 2022) that such clauses are void. § 16600.5 (SB 699) says a contract void under § 16600 is unenforceable "regardless of where and when the contract was signed," makes entering or attempting to enforce such a contract a civil violation, and gives employees a private right of action for injunctive relief, damages, and attorney's fees. AB 1076 also codified that § 16600 is read broadly per Edwards. Sources: https://www.ebglaw.com/insights/publications/california-makes-its-strict-noncompete-law-even-stricter ; https://www.goodwinlaw.com/en/insights/publications/2024/01/alerts-practices-tsemnc-new-laws-reinforce-californias-hostility-to-non-competes-with-notice ; https://www.quinnemanuel.com/media/3b1nrqhz/client-alert_-new-california-non-compete-legislation.pdf ; https://www.sgrlaw.com/client-alerts/two-new-laws-expand-californias-non-compete-prohibition-and-require-employers-to-provide-notice-to-current-and-former-employees/

### Labor Code § 432.5
An employer may not require an employee or applicant to agree in writing to any term or condition "known by him or her to be prohibited by law." It is a short statute often paired with § 16600 arguments. Penalties can be pursued under the PAGA framework (§ 2699). Sources: https://www.shrm.org/in/topics-tools/employment-law-compliance/employer-beware-12-california-labor-code-requirements-dont-even-know-exist ; https://content.seyfarth.com/publications/Cal-Peculiarities-2024/271 ; https://californiaglobe.com/fr/contracts-and-applications-for-employment-under-the-labor-code/

### Civil Code § 1542 (releases of unknown claims)
A general release "does not extend to claims that the creditor or releasing party does not know or suspect to exist in his or her favor at the time of executing the release and that, if known by him or her, would have materially affected his or her settlement." To release unknown claims, California practice is to quote the statute and have the releasor expressly waive it. The statutory wording was amended effective 2019, so older forms may be out of date. Separately, Labor Code § 206.5 bars releases of wages concededly due, though a release of a bona fide disputed wage claim is allowed (Watkins v. Wachovia). Sources: https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2019/2019-02-05-california-legislature-amends-required-ccp-1542-language ; https://www.mccormickbarstow.com/changes-to-civil-code-section-1542-could-mean-your-severance-agreements-are-out-dated/ ; https://blog.swlaw.com/labor-and-employment/?p=477 ; https://www.weintraub.com/2009/07/law-alert-can-an-employee-release-a-wage-claim-it-depends-is-there-a-bona-fide-dispute/

### Older Workers Benefit Protection Act (29 U.S.C. § 626(f))
A waiver of federal age-discrimination (ADEA) claims by someone 40 or older is valid only if "knowing and voluntary." The statute lists minimum terms: plain-language writing; specific reference to the ADEA; no waiver of future claims; consideration beyond what is already owed; written advice to consult an attorney; at least 21 days to consider (45 days for a group program, plus written disclosure of the group's ages and titles); and at least 7 days after signing to revoke. The 7-day revocation cannot be shortened or waived; an employee may sign early if not pressured. Text: https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/29/626 ; summaries: https://www.wilentz.com/blog/employment/2025-08-12-21-days-or-45-days-how-many-days-does-an-employee-have-to-review-a-separation-agreement/_pdfx/2025-08-12-21-days-or-45-days-how-many-days-does-an-employee-have-to-review-a-separation-agreement.pdf ; https://www.lexplug.com/outlines/employment-law/owbpa-waivers-of-age-discrimination-claims

### IRC § 409A (deferred compensation; severance timing)
§ 409A taxes "nonqualified deferred compensation" that lacks fixed payment timing, with an extra 20% federal tax (California adds its own 5% under state conformity; Cooley cites "an additional 25% for CA residents" combined) on violations. Severance can be exempt as a "short-term deferral" (paid within 2.5 months after year-end of vesting) or under the "separation pay" exception (involuntary termination, up to two times the lesser of annual pay or the 401(a)(17) limit, paid by the end of the second year). A "good reason" resignation counts as involuntary only if it fits the regulations' safe harbor (material diminution in pay, authority, duties; material relocation; employer breach; notice within 90 days, 30-day cure, resignation within two years). "Specified employees" of public companies face a six-month payment delay on non-exempt amounts. A release condition must not let the executive choose the tax year: IRS Notices 2010-6 and 2010-80 require either a fixed payment date (e.g., 60th day) or payment in the second year when the window spans two years. Sources: https://www.wilmerhale.com/insights/publications/severance-pay-under-the-final-section-409a-regulations-may-25-2007 ; https://www.fenwick.com/insights/publications/executive-compensation-alert-relief-period-for-section-409a-release-timing-errors-expires-on-december-31-2012-action-needed ; https://www.mcgrathnorth.com/publications/applying-code-section-409a-to-severance-agreements-or-not ; Cooley CompTalks (Aug. 2019) p. on 409A: https://www.cooley.com/-/media/cooley/pdf/comptalks-change-in-control-and-severance-compensation-august-2019.pdf

### IRC §§ 280G and 4999 (golden parachutes)
A "parachute payment" is compensation to a "disqualified individual" (officers, 1%+ shareholders, top-paid 1% or 250 employees) that is contingent on a change in ownership or control, if the aggregate present value equals or exceeds 3 times the "base amount" (average W-2 pay over the prior five years). If the 3x line is crossed, everything above 1x base amount is an "excess parachute payment": the company loses its deduction (§ 280G) and the executive pays a 20% excise tax (§ 4999) on top of ordinary tax. Private companies can cleanse payments by a 75% disinterested shareholder vote. Agreements handle this with a gross-up (company pays the tax, now rare and disfavored by proxy advisors), a hard cutback to just under 3x, or a "best-net" / "better-after-tax" clause that pays whichever of full or cut-back yields more after taxes. Text: https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/280G ; explanations: https://policyrisk.com/wiki/golden-parachute-280g ; https://www.morganlewis.com/-/media/files/publication/presentation/webinar/eb_changeincontrolwebinar_23june11.pdf ; Cooley CompTalks (lists "excise tax gross-up protection" and "single-trigger acceleration" among features proxy advisors flag): https://www.cooley.com/-/media/cooley/pdf/comptalks-change-in-control-and-severance-compensation-august-2019.pdf

### California's Armendariz standard (mandatory arbitration)
Armendariz v. Foundation Health Psychcare Services, Inc., 24 Cal.4th 83 (2000) holds that a mandatory, employer-imposed arbitration clause can cover unwaivable statutory claims (e.g., FEHA) only if it (1) provides a neutral arbitrator, (2) allows adequate discovery, (3) permits all remedies available in court, (4) requires a written award allowing limited review, and (5) does not require the employee to pay arbitration costs beyond what court would cost. The agreement also needs a "modicum of bilaterality"; one-sided clauses are substantively unconscionable. Later cases apply the same frame: OTO v. Kho (2019) (procedural + substantive unconscionability), Ramirez v. Charter Communications (2024) (fee-shifting, one-sided claim coverage, shortened limitations periods can be unconscionable; courts must do a qualitative severance analysis rather than count defects). Sources: https://scocal.stanford.edu/opinion/armendariz-v-foundation-health-psychcare-services-inc-32092 ; https://scocal.stanford.edu/opinion/oto-llc-v-kho-34655 ; https://courts.ca.gov/opinions/documents/S273802.PDF ; https://www.gibsondunn.com/california-supreme-court-holds-that-courts-must-conduct-qualitative-severance-analysis-even-if-they-find-multiple-unconscionable-provisions-in-an-arbitration-agreement/

### Ending Forced Arbitration of Sexual Assault and Sexual Harassment Act (EFAA), Pub. L. 117-90 (Mar. 3, 2022)
Adds 9 U.S.C. §§ 401–402. At the election of the person alleging a "sexual harassment dispute" or "sexual assault dispute," no predispute arbitration agreement or joint-action waiver is enforceable "with respect to a case" relating to that dispute. Applies to disputes or claims that arise or accrue on or after March 3, 2022. Courts, not arbitrators, decide applicability. California appellate courts have read it broadly: Kader (2024) held a "dispute" arises when a party asserts a claim (e.g., an agency charge), not when the conduct occurred; Liu v. Miniso Depot (2024) held that one covered harassment claim keeps the entire case in court. The Supreme Court declined review in Miniso. Sources: https://www.congress.gov/117/plaws/publ90/PLAW-117publ90.htm ; https://www.fenwick.com/insights/publications/federal-arbitration-act-amended-to-prohibit-mandatory-arbitration-of-sexual-assault-and-sexual-harassment-claims ; https://calemploymentlawupdate.proskauer.com/2024/03/dispute-does-not-exist-under-ending-forced-arbitration-act-until-employee-asserts-a-claim-or-demand/ ; https://calemploymentlawupdate.proskauer.com/2024/12/solitary-sexual-harassment-claim-shields-entire-lawsuit-from-arbitration/ ; https://www.thompsoncoburn.com/insights/u-s-supreme-court-declines-to-address-a-significant-efaa-issue-102lqnr/

### Delaware law relevance (Delaware corporation, California executive)
The agreement picks California law, so Delaware law mostly matters through the "internal affairs doctrine" (Delaware governs matters among the corporation, its directors, officers, and stockholders) and through DGCL § 145 (indemnification and advancement of legal fees for officers and directors; a separate indemnification agreement can go beyond bylaws, and D&O insurance is how most companies fund it). California courts have held the internal affairs doctrine does not override a California choice-of-law clause in an officer's employment agreement (Colaco v. Cavotec SA, Cal. Ct. App. 2018), and Delaware has said the doctrine does not reach an entity's relationship with its employees (McCarter summary). Delaware's Chancery Court has itself refused to apply a Delaware choice-of-law clause to a California employee's non-compete (Ascension Insurance v. Underwood, 2015), but enforced one where the executive was represented by counsel under § 925(e) (NuVasive v. Miles, 2018/2019). Delaware's Supreme Court also now declines to "blue-pencil" overbroad covenants (Sunder Energy v. Jackson, 2024). In the Masimo dispute, Chancery dismissed the company's suit in favor of the California forum named in the CEO's employment agreement (Apr. 21, 2026). Sources: https://www.calcorporatelaw.com/california-court-applies-california-law-to-officer-of-delaware-corporation ; https://www.mccarter.com/insights/court-of-chancery-rejects-internal-affairs-doctrine-in-employment-related-lawsuit/ ; https://businesslawtoday.org/2021/03/delaware-versus-california-choice-law-juul-labs-inc-v-grove/ ; https://www.delawarelitigation.com/2022/02/articles/commentary/amendments-to-dgcl-section-145-indemnification-and-advancement/ ; https://www.faegredrinker.com/en/insights/publications/2022/6/am-i-covered-understanding-the-scope-and-availability-of-directors-and-officers--indemnification-and ; https://corpgov.law.harvard.edu/2019/10/13/delaware-choice-of-law-provisions-in-restrictive-covenant-agreements ; https://www.sheppard.com/insights/blogs/delaware-supreme-court-refuses-to-enforce-noncompete-against-company-founder-who-joined-competitor ; https://www.fenwick.com/sites/default/files/2026-07/Masimo%20Corp%20%20v.%20Kiani%20-%20Delaware%20Chancery%20-%20Opinion-%20%202026-c-a-no-2024-1086-nac.pdf

## 4. What AAA employment arbitration looks like for an executive

### Rules set
- Current rules: AAA "Employment/Workplace Arbitration Rules and Mediation Procedures," amended and effective May 1, 2025 (a 2026 version also appears on adr.org). The rules incorporate the "Employment Due Process Protocol." https://www.adr.org/media/0vrpbnm0/2025_employment_arbitration_rules.pdf ; https://www.adr.org/media/aktbpg2y/2026_employment-arbitration-rules-and-mediation-procedures.pdf
- Rule R-1(c): AAA decides at intake whether the Employment/Workplace Rules and Fee Schedule apply; objections go to the arbitrator. A footnote states that a dispute from an agreement that does not meet the R-1(b) definition "will be administered under the AAA Rules and fee schedule applicable to the dispute as determined by the AAA." (Rules text, p. 5–6.)
- AAA classifies cases as arising from an "employer-promulgated plan" or an "individually-negotiated employment agreement"; the fee schedule differs. Summary (law-firm and AAA sources): for employer plans the individual's filing fee is capped at $350 and the employer pays the rest plus arbitrator compensation; for individually negotiated agreements the employee's initial filing fee is $750 and the employee pays one-half of hearing fees and one-half of arbitrator compensation unless the agreement or law says otherwise. https://adr.org/sites/default/files/document_repository/Employment%20Fee%20Schedule.pdf ; https://www.adr.org/sites/default/files/document_repository/Educational%20Presentation%20-%20Employment.pdf ; https://hrdailyadvisor.hci.org/?p=11905

### Arbitrator selection (Rule R-13, 2025 rules)
- AAA sends each side an identical list of arbitrators from its Employment Panel. Parties may agree on one; if not, each has 14 calendar days to strike names and rank the rest. Lists are not exchanged. A party who does not return the list is deemed to accept all names. AAA may limit strikes and may supplement from its National Roster. If no mutual choice is possible, AAA appoints. (Rules text, R-13(b), p. 12.)

### Typical timeline
- AAA's Employment Arbitration Road Map: Day 1–15 AAA commences administration; Day 15–35 arbitrator selection; Day 35–75 preliminary management conference. https://www.adr.org/sites/default/files/document_repository/AAA237_Employment_Arbitration_Road_Map.pdf
- AAA 2024 Employment Infographic: median time to award 16.6 months (fastest 2.4 months) versus 31.6 months median time to trial in U.S. District Court; 76% of cases closed in 2024 settled before award; 377 cases reached award vs. 2,640 settled. https://www.adr.org/sites/default/files/document_repository/2024_Employment_Infographic.pdf
- Colvin (2003–2007 AAA data): mean time to disposition 284.4 days for settled cases and 361.5 days for cases decided after hearing. https://ecommons.cornell.edu/items/25fabf00-b71e-460d-b7a3-bf60d7fd1e0f/full

### Cost allocation
- AAA rules: arbitrator compensation is paid per the fee schedule; the arbitrator may award administrative fees or arbitrator compensation against a business only as applicable law requires or if the claim was filed to harass or is patently frivolous (R-46(c)). The arbitrator may award attorney's fees and costs "in accordance with applicable law" (R-46(a)). (Rules text, p. 24.)
- California law (Armendariz) independently requires the employer to bear arbitration costs unique to the forum when it imposes arbitration on unwaivable statutory claims. https://scocal.stanford.edu/opinion/armendariz-v-foundation-health-psychcare-services-inc-32092
- CCP §§ 1281.97–1281.98 require the drafting employer to pay invoiced fees within 30 days or risk material breach and waiver; Hohenshelt (2025) limits forfeiture to willful, grossly negligent, or fraudulent nonpayment. https://www.dlapiper.com/insights/publications/2025/08/california-supreme-court-clarifies-faa-preemption
- AAA 2024 data: in 1,747 cases employees paid no administrative fee; AAA caps employee fees at $350 under employer plans. https://www.adr.org/sites/default/files/document_repository/2024_Employment_Infographic.pdf

### Employee win rates in published studies
- Colvin, "An Empirical Study of Employment Arbitration" (J. Empirical Legal Studies 2011; 3,945 AAA cases 2003–2007, 1,213 awards): employee win rate 21.4%; median award $36,500; mean $109,858. https://ecommons.cornell.edu/items/25fabf00-b71e-460d-b7a3-bf60d7fd1e0f/full ; https://www.consumeradvocates.org/sites/default/files/AnImpericalSTudy_0.pdf
- Colvin, EPI (2018): 56.2% of private-sector nonunion workers are under mandatory arbitration; employees win 21% in arbitration vs. 57% in state court; awards about $109,000 vs. $575,000. https://www.epi.org/publication/the-growing-use-of-mandatory-arbitration/
- Salary effect (Colvin data as summarized by Zuckerman Spaeder): win rates 22.7% (<$100K), 31.4% ($100K–$250K), 42.9% (>$250K). Gough (discrimination cases): 45% arbitration vs. 63% litigation win rate; awards roughly half. O'Hara O'Connor et al. (2012): 52% of 910 CEO contracts had arbitration clauses, with parties customizing arbitrator selection and cost terms but rarely discovery, confidentiality, or appeal. https://3e.zuckerman.com/news/insightzs/should-executives-arbitrate-empiricists-weigh
- AAA cautions that its own infographic does not report win rates by party. https://www.adr.org/sites/default/files/document_repository/2024_Employment_Infographic.pdf
