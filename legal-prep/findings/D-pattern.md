Legacy-form details the 2026 proxy states in its own words (raw text checked):

- Legacy severance "benefits ... are coupled with non-competition obligations that might not be enforceable in the absence of additional consideration."
- Legacy CIC trigger: termination without cause or for good reason "within 18 months after (i) a change of control or (ii) for officers other than Mr. Balakrishnan, the date that Mr. Balakrishnan ceased to serve as Chief Executive Officer." Mr. Balakrishnan ceased to be CEO on July 21, 2025.
- Legacy "senior executive" = at least five years of continuous service as an executive officer; "new executive" = fewer than five. New executives get 6 months / 0.5x; senior executives get up to a further 6 months salary and 50% of annual PSUs at maximum "until such senior executive secures new employment."
- Legacy retirement benefits require age/service, "is not employed elsewhere, full time (other than for an organization described in section 501(c)(3) of the Code), or otherwise engaged in 'Competition' (as defined in the EOBA) or in material breach of ... the executive's confidentiality agreement."
- The company "do[es] not provide any tax gross-up payments on severance benefits."

Observed patterns at Power Integrations (facts only):

1. Terminations without cause (Torres 2008, Roeschlein 2010, Bailey 2025) paid exactly the form: six months salary plus half the target bonus plus six months COBRA, in exchange for a release.
2. Voluntary departures for another job (Renouard 2011, Yee 2024, Nayyar 2025, Hollister-type moves at peers) disclosed no payments.
3. Retirements that met the age/service test (Barsan 2025) paid the retirement benefit (prorated performance awards) plus a token cash recognition. The retirement benefit in the legacy form is conditioned on not working full time elsewhere and not being in "Competition."
4. The one long-tenured CEO exit was structured as transition employment then unpaid consulting, with equity continuing to vest, in exchange for striking the cash severance, COBRA and retirement provisions and widening "Cause." The company's own 10-K says the consulting does not count as substantive service under ASC 718.
5. Individual deviations from the form were negotiated at hire or promotion (Nayyar one-year wait then partial waiver; Gupta "deemed Senior Executive"; Erba 30-minute relocation trigger and 1x CIC multiple; Torres option exercise tweak).
6. A role change at the executive's own request ended EOBA eligibility (Sutherland 2021).
7. The company moved to a new severance form in mid-2025 and describes the legacy form's retirement/non-compete coupling as possibly unenforceable "in the absence of additional consideration."

Patterns across peers (facts only):

- Retirements by long-tenured CFOs/COOs were commonly paired with a short consulting tail (6–18 months) and some form of extra vesting (Walsh, Vig, Teebagy, Maheswaran, Chukwu). The consulting was usually unpaid in cash; its function was to keep equity vesting.
- Terminations without cause paid the pre-existing contract (Lowe, Nargolwala, Bailey at POWI). In Lowe's case the board asked the executive to give back part of what the contract promised, and he did.
- Several companies replaced or renewed severance agreements that had fixed expiry dates (Silicon Labs 2024; ADI/Maxim 2023; Semtech CIC plan extended to 2029 in 2024 ([8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/88941/000008894124000083/smtc-20240608.htm))).
- A CEO transition prompted a time-limited retention/severance agreement for a key lieutenant at Semtech (Silberstein, protection through Dec 8, 2024) and, at POWI, the legacy form itself already treated the CEO's departure as a CIC-style 18-month window.
- Interim officers were kept with milestone cash plus a pre-agreed severance (Wolfspeed Speirits), while POWI's interim CFO had no EOBA at all.

## 5. Other countries, only where they show a pattern

**United Kingdom — settlement agreements.** Under s.203(3) Employment Rights Act 1996 a settlement agreement waiving statutory claims is binding only if the employee received advice from a "relevant independent adviser" (a qualified lawyer, certified union official or advice-centre worker) on "the terms and effect of the agreement"; the adviser must be insured and named in the agreement. Employers are not required to pay for it but usually contribute (commonly ~£500 plus VAT; higher for senior executives, with one firm quoting £1,250–£2,500 plus VAT for executives with incentive schemes). Sources: [Birketts, 20 Feb 2025](https://www.birketts.co.uk/legal-update/the-importance-of-legal-advice-on-settlement-agreements); [Torque Law fee guide](https://torquelaw.co.uk/fee-guide-for-settlement-agreement-advice-for-employees-and-executives/); [SE Solicitors](https://www.se-solicitors.co.uk/?p=24013).
Pattern (two lines): The UK system treats independent advice as a condition of validity, not a courtesy, and the employer pays for it. The cost of advice scales with the complexity of the executive's incentive arrangements.

**Germany — Aufhebungsvertrag (termination agreement).** The Federal Labour Court recognises a "Gebot fairen Verhandelns" (duty of fair negotiation) as a side duty under §241(2) BGB: BAG 7 Feb 2019, 6 AZR 75/18, and BAG 24 Feb 2022, 6 AZR 333/21. The 2022 ruling held that making the offer conditional on immediate acceptance, with no time to reflect or consult a lawyer, is not by itself a breach; a breach needs circumstances that substantially impair free decision-making (hidden purpose of the meeting, hours of isolation, exploiting an evident disability or language barrier, surprise timing or location). Sources: [Gleiss Lutz (English)](https://www.gleisslutz.com/en/node/3363); [DATEV magazine](https://www.datev-magazin.de/nachrichten-steuern-recht/recht/aufhebungsvertrag-gebot-fairen-verhandelns-74282); [Advant Beiten](https://www.advant-beiten.com/aktuelles/bag-aufhebungsvertrag-gebot-fairen-verhandelns).
Pattern (two lines): Even in a system with a named fairness duty, a short deadline alone does not invalidate the signed document; courts look at the whole setting. The protection is narrow and after-the-fact.
