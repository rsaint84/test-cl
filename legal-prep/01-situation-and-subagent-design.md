## 2. What the chat must tell us before the type is certain

The title says "executive agreement." That phrase covers five different animals. Each is handled by a different lawyer and shaped by different cases.

| Possible type | What it would mean | What would confirm it |
|---|---|---|
| Offer or hiring agreement | You are joining or being promoted | A start date, a title, equity grants |
| Executive Officer Benefits Agreement | Their standard severance and change-of-control form | The phrase "Benefits Agreement," Section 21 definitions |
| Retention or change-of-control agreement | Someone expects a deal | Acceleration terms, a transaction trigger |
| Separation or release agreement | You are leaving | A release of claims, a 21-day or 45-day review window |
| Consulting or advisory agreement | Post-employment work | An independent contractor clause, hourly or project pay |

Until the chat is read, the sub-agents below are built to cover all five. The moment the type is confirmed, they narrow.

## 3. The four sub-agents

Each one owns a different wall of the same room. None overlap. Each must report what it found, what it could not find, and what it doubts.

### Sub-agent A: The Document
- Reads the agreement, or the chat's description of it, clause by clause.
- Names the agreement type from the list above.
- Pulls Power Integrations' public versions of the same form from SEC filings, 2010 to 2025.
- Builds a side-by-side table: your version against every public version.
- Flags every clause where your version is weaker, stronger, or missing.
- Lists every defined term: Cause, Good Reason, Change of Control, Release, Retirement.

### Sub-agent B: The Law
- Finds which kind of lawyer handles this type of agreement for the executive side in California.
- Finds the rules that govern it: California Labor Code, the Business and Professions Code non-compete ban, Section 409A, Section 280G, arbitration law.
- Finds the cases where executives won on these clauses, and the cases where they lost.
- Extracts from each case what the winning side did, and what the losing side missed.
- Finds what AAA arbitration in San Jose actually looks like for executives.

### Sub-agent C: The People
- Builds a profile of every person on the other side of the table.
- The CEO: her background, her deals at Analog Devices, her first year at Power Integrations, her public statements.
- The executive chairman and the board: who sits on compensation, what they have approved before.
- The CFO and general counsel: who signs, who negotiates.
- Your own lawyers: their firm, their practice area, their record in executive matters, what they are known for.
- Nothing invented. Public record only. Where the record is silent, the report says so.

### Sub-agent D: The Pattern
- Studies how other executives at Power Integrations negotiated the same form, using filings that show amendments.
- Studies how executives at peer companies handled the same form: Monolithic Power, Vicor, Navitas, Wolfspeed, Analog Devices.
- Finds forums, interviews, and write-ups where executives describe what they pushed for and what they regretted.
- Finds the common mistakes: signing on the company's timeline, accepting the standard Good Reason, missing the release window.
- Reports which patterns match your position and which do not.

### How they talk to each other
- Each sub-agent writes its findings to a shared file before it finishes.
- Each one then reads the other three files and adds a short note: agree, disagree, or missing.
- Disagreements come back to you unresolved. They are not smoothed over.

## 4. The first five questions

Plain. Simple. In the order they must be answered.

1. **What do we have?** What exact document is on the table, and which of the five types is it?
2. **What is the standard?** What does Power Integrations' public form say, and how does yours differ?
3. **Who are we dealing with?** Who is across the table, and what have they done before?
4. **Who is on our side?** Who are your lawyers, what do they handle, and what is their record?
5. **Who has done this before?** Which executives faced this same form, and what happened to them?

Question one depends on the chat. Questions two through five can start without it.
